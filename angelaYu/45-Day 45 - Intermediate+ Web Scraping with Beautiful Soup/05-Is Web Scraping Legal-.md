Here is a structured breakdown of this lesson on the legality of web scraping.

---

### 1. The Short Answer

Scraping **public** data for personal, non-commercial use is generally fine; the legal
grey areas start when you take *other people's* content or burden *their servers*.

---

### 2. The Four Rules of Polite Scraping

| Concern | What it means |
|---------|---------------|
| **robots.txt** | `site.com/robots.txt` declares what bots may access — respect it |
| **Terms of Service** | some sites explicitly forbid scraping (e.g. LinkedIn) |
| **Copyright** | data ≠ yours to republish; personal use ≠ commercial reuse |
| **Server load** | add delays, cache results — scraping ≠ DDoS |

> **Warning:** Scraping behind a login or in violation of ToS can breach computer-misuse
> laws. Google/Bing scrape because they pay for the infrastructure that makes the web work.

---

### Summary Checklist

1. Public + personal + polite = acceptable; private + commercial + abusive = trouble.
2. Check robots.txt and ToS before scraping any new site.
