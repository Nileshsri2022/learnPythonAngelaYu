Here is a structured breakdown of this "Study With Me" session.

---

### 1. What It Is

A recorded focus session — the instructor works alongside you in real time, usually in
25–50 minute blocks with short breaks. The message is simple: **show up, put the phone
away, and code**.

---

### 2. Why It Works

| Ingredient | Effect |
|------------|--------|
| A visible timer | makes the session finite — start is easier than "work all day" |
| Someone else working | social accountability, even when it's a recording |
| No talking, no tutorial | you're *doing*, not watching |
| Scheduled breaks | keeps attention fresh across a longer day |

---

### 3. A Practical Recipe

1. **Pre-decide the task** *before* starting — "build the login route", not "work on the
   project".
2. **25 minutes, one task, no tabs.** Timer visible.
3. **5-minute break.** Stand up. Water. Not social media.
4. Repeat. After 3–4 blocks take a longer break.
5. **Log what you did** in one line: "Fixed the 404 on /post/<id>; tomorrow: add images."

> **Tip:** Consistency beats intensity. Thirty focused minutes every day will finish the
> portfolio projects long before one heroic 8-hour Saturday.

---

### 4. Removing Friction

* Leave the editor open on the exact file you'll work on next.
* Keep a "next action" note so you never start with a blank stare.
* Phone in another room (not just face-down).
* Noise-cancelling headphones or a fixed soundtrack as a "start working" cue.

---

### 5. If You Miss a Day

Don't compound it. Do ten minutes instead of zero — the streak of *showing up* matters more
than the length of any single session. Momentum is the asset.

---

### Summary Checklist

1. Study-with-me sessions work by making focus finite and visible.
2. Decide the task, run a timer, break properly, log one line.
3. Daily short sessions beat occasional marathons.
4. Remove friction and keep a "next action" so starting is trivial.
