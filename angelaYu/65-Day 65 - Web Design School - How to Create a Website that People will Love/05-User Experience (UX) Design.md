# User Experience (UX) Design

---

### 1. What UX Really Is

The Reed College story: an architect leaves the campus as pure lawn, returns a year later,
and paves the paths students *actually* wore into the grass.

* **UI/design** is what you think looks good and impose on users.
* **UX** is invisible — a good experience feels effortless and lets people do what they
  already wanted to do.

---

### 2. The Five UX Principles

| Principle | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Simplicity** | less is more | Monocle (calm, curated news) vs Sina (dense link soup) |
| **Consistency** | same look *and* same behaviour everywhere | buttons that always do the same thing |
| **Hierarchy** | the most important action is the most obvious | "Express delivery" highlighted |
| **Feedback** | users must know something happened | loading spinners, confirmations |
| **Forgiveness** | make mistakes easy to undo | clear "cancel"/"undo", confirmations |

When choosing between more complex and simpler: **always pare it down.**

---

### 3. Dark Patterns: UX Used for Evil

Good UX helps users do what they want. Dark patterns trick them into something else:

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Misleading emphasis | highlighting the expensive "Express delivery" so you "choose" it |
| Fake affordances | a one-pixel curved line that looks like a hair on the screen — tapping "removes" it and clicks the ad |
| Hidden opt-outs | Ryanair burying "No travel insurance" deep inside an alphabetical dropdown (below "Denmark") |
| Confusing double negatives | "If you would like us to no longer continue to stop not sending you offers, indicate yes by not checking the box" |

> **⚠️ Warning:** These tricks may raise short-term revenue, but they destroy trust — the
> user never comes back. Design for the user, not against them.

---

### 4. UX in Practice

* Reduce the number of clicks and decisions.
* Keep interactions in the same place across pages.
* Give feedback for every action.
* Never make "no" harder to click than "yes".
* Check that your copy can't be misread — especially checkbox wording.

---

### Summary Checklist

1. UX should be invisible; if users notice the interface, something is wrong.
2. Five principles: simplicity, consistency, hierarchy, feedback, forgiveness.
3. Dark patterns trade trust for a click — know them so you never ship them.
4. Every action needs feedback, and every opt-out must be as easy as opting in.
