# Web Design in Practice - Let's apply what we've learnt!

---

### 1. The Exercise

Design a **three-page hotel website** from scratch in Canva, applying:

* colour theory,
* typography,
* UI design (hierarchy, layout, alignment, white space, audience),
* UX design (simplicity, consistency, hierarchy, feedback, forgiveness).

Pages: home, rooms, contact/book.

---

### 2. Sketch Before You Code

Designing in a tool like Canva is fast — no HTML, no CSS, no deploy. Sketching the design
elsewhere lets you explore and throw away ideas cheaply, which is exactly what prototypes
are for.

---

### 3. Publishing a Design in Canva

* *Publish* → scroll to *Website* → choose the **Standard** web style.
* Canva stitches your pages into a scrollable site with a working nav bar.
* Limitations: no mobile optimisation, no real functionality, no custom JavaScript.

Good enough to show someone the *feel* of the design and get feedback before writing a line
of code.

---

### 4. Apply the Pillars

| Decision | Guidance |
|----------|----------|
| Colour | pick a dominant colour that matches the hotel's story (sea blue for a beach resort, gold/deep red for an old, decadent hotel) |
| Type | two fonts: one serif for headings (expense, tradition), one sans-serif for body text (readability) |
| UI | hero image with one obvious "Book now" action; generous white space; consistent alignment; 40–60 character lines |
| UX | three taps to book; same nav on every page; clear prices; no dark patterns |

---

### 5. Practise Deliberately

* Collect sites you love; write down *why* each works (palette? type? hierarchy?).
* Redesign the same brief in a different mood — e.g. the same hotel as a minimalist
  modern property vs a Henry VIII-era banquet hall.
* Share designs and invite critique; iteration is how design skill grows.

> **Tip:** Design is one of the highest-leverage skills in web development — it makes your
> code *look* as good as it works.

---

### Summary Checklist

1. Prototype in Canva before coding; Canva can publish a clickable site.
2. Prototypes are for feedback on feel and flow, not for functionality.
3. Every decision traces back to one of the four pillars.
4. Study good websites, name what makes them good, and copy the technique — not the pixels.
