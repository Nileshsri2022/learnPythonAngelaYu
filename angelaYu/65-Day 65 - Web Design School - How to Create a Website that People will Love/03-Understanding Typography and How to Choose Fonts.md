Here is a structured breakdown of this lesson on typography.

---

### 1. Fonts Carry a Mood

A Valentine's letter in a ransom-note font says something very different from the same
words in a cursive script. Fonts are not decorative — they set tone before a single word is
understood.

---

### 2. The Two Big Families

| Family | Look | Feels | Best for |
|--------|------|-------|----------|
| **Serif** | little "feet" on the strokes | traditional, stable, respectable, authoritative, older | legal letterheads, Vogue-style magazines, wineries |
| **Sans-serif** | clean right angles | friendly, approachable, novel, contemporary, simple | startups, UI, body text on screen |

Serifs come from Roman stone carving — you can't cut a 90° angle with a chisel, so the
stroke flares out.

**Sub-families of serif:** Old Style → Transitional → Modern → Slab-Serif. The difference
between the thickest and thinnest parts of a letter grows as you move towards modern
(Bodoni/Didot = modern; Garamond = old style).

---

### 3. Legibility vs Readability

Legibility is the form of the letters; readability is how pleasant a block of text is to
read.

**Legible letters have:** open shapes, generous spacing, unambiguous forms (a `g` that
can't be confused with a `9`, a wide `O` vs a narrow `0`).

MIT tracked drivers' pupils and found a Humanist sans-serif dashboard was easier on the
eyes than a Grotesque one — comfort matters even at a glance.

---

### 4. Combining Fonts

Rules of thumb:

1. **Use at most two fonts** per design (one more always looks cluttered).
2. Pick fonts with **similar moods and the same era** — don't mix ultra-modern with
   medieval.
3. Create contrast in **structure**: serif heading + sans-serif body (or vice versa).
4. Or contrast in **weight**: black/bold heading + light/regular body.

---

### 5. Six Fonts to Avoid

The notorious defaults (Comic Sans, Papyrus, Curlz, Impact, Bradley Hand, Trajan) are hard
to read and carry moods that rarely fit a professional context. Fine for a lemonade stand;
not for a brand.

> **Tip:** Before shipping, ask: what am I trying to convey — authority, friendliness,
> luxury? Then choose the family that says it, and limit yourself to two.

---

### Summary Checklist

1. Serif = traditional/authoritative; sans-serif = modern/friendly.
2. Readability matters: open shapes, spacing, unambiguous characters.
3. Two fonts maximum, same mood and era, contrasted by structure or weight.
4. Avoid the six infamous defaults in professional designs.
5. Choose fonts to match your message, not your personal favourite.
