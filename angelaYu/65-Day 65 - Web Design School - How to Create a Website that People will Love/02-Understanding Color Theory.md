# Understanding Color Theory

---

### 1. Colour Choice Is a Decision, Not a Preference

The predominant colour tells a story about your brand. Flip a bad palette and the same logo
suddenly makes sense: the flowers were brown ("dirt"), the paintball pink ("who is this
for?") — swap them and it reads as intentional.

---

### 2. What Colours Say

| Colour | Mood / message | Seen in |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| **Red** | love, energy, intensity | car ads, food brands |
| **Yellow** | joy, intellect, attention | warnings, headlines — impossible to ignore |
| **Green** | freshness, growth, safety | groceries (HelloFresh), health |
| **Blue** | stability, trust, serenity | banks, PayPal, Coinbase |
| **Purple** | royalty, wealth, femininity | luxury, "premium" offers |

> **Tip:** Yellow grabs attention but is exhausting as a background — great for a headline,
> terrible for a full page of body text. (The "beach body ready" ad is a masterclass in
> yellow's ability to hijack attention.)

---

### 3. Combining Colours (the scientific bit)

| Scheme | How to build it | Good for | Not good for |
|--------|-----------------|----------|--------------|
| **Analogous** | two neighbours on the colour wheel | harmony: nav + body, logo + background | standing out |
| **Complementary** | two opposite colours | pop: logos, icons, "buy" buttons | text on text |
| **Triadic** | equilateral triangle on the wheel | playful, balanced variety | subtle designs |
| **Square** | perfect square on the wheel | four-way palettes | quick decisions |

The supermarket trick: green plastic grass between red meat — complementary colours make
the meat look fresher.

> **⚠️ Warning:** Never put complementary colours together for *text* — red on green fuzzes
> the eyes and is genuinely painful to read.

---

### 4. Tools

* **Adobe Color** — drag palettes around the wheel, copy the hex codes.
* **Color Hunt** — curated palettes from professional designers, ready to steal.
* Consistency is the point: the Liebe yogurt site works because every colour belongs.

---

### 5. Practical Rules

1. Pick one dominant colour that matches your brand's message.
2. Add one or two supporting colours using a scheme above.
3. Use the accent colour only where you want attention (buttons, links).
4. Keep it consistent across every page and component.

---

### Summary Checklist

1. Colour = mood: red energy, yellow attention, green fresh, blue trust, purple luxury.
2. Analogous = harmonious; complementary = high contrast; triadic/square = variety.
3. Complementary pairs are for logos/icons, never for text.
4. Use Adobe Color / Color Hunt and always think "what message?" not "what's my favourite?".
